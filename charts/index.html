About The WHD

A curated, searchable catalog of women's health variables across major cohort studies worldwide.

Women's health variables are critical to understanding health and aging, yet what is measured — and how it is measured — varies substantially across cohorts. The Women's Health Database catalogs variables collected in 29 major cohorts across 11 domains of women's health, spanning menstrual and reproductive history, gynecologic conditions and surgeries, pregnancy and fertility, menopause, hormone therapy, and linked cognitive, imaging, biological, and health-record data.

Our goal is to make it easier to find the right data, understand what those data can tell us, and improve how women's health is measured in future research.

Hysterectomy Inference Framework

Our first application of the Women's Health Database focuses on hysterectomy and oophorectomy. We developed a five-type framework that characterizes the inferences about hysterectomy and ovarian status that can be supported by the information available in each cohort.

Explore the Database

Use the database to identify cohorts with the measures needed for your research, compare ascertainment across studies, and evaluate the strength and limitations of the inferences that the data can support.

Items Reference

Every variable The WHD tracks, grouped into the same 11 domains used throughout the site. This page doesn't show any cohort data itself — it's a glossary of what each variable means. Click a domain to expand it.

These same domains, and the individual items that make each of them up, can be filtered directly in — check a domain there to see it as a single rollup column, or drill into its specific items to see which cohorts actually collect each one.

1. Menstrual History
  • Age at menarche
  • Menstrual cycle / bleeding pattern
    • Cycle regularity
    • Cycle length in days
    • Change in cycle length
    • Skipped cycles
    • Time since last period
    • Length of time without a period (amenorrhea)
    • Bleeding flow / amount
    • Abnormal vaginal bleeding — bleeding from the vagina that is different enough from a normal pattern to be a concern: irregular, heavy, or long in duration
2. Reproductive Surgical History
  • Is there a question directly asking about hysterectomy?
  • Is there a question directly asking about oophorectomy?
    • If oophorectomy is asked about: does it distinguish unilateral vs. bilateral (laterality)?
  • Does it distinguish hysterectomy type (supracervical / total / radical)?
  • Is age at surgery recorded?
  • Is indication of surgery recorded (e.g., cancer, benign condition)?
  • Is surgery captured at baseline only, or also as incident events during follow-up? (Baseline only / Incident / Not applicable)
  • Was intact uterine and/or ovarian status used as an enrollment eligibility criterion?
3. Gynecologic Conditions
  • Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) / Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS)
  • Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI)
  • Endometriosis
  • Pelvic prolapse or relaxation
  • Pelvic cancer (cancer of the vulva, cervix, uterus, or ovaries)
  • Fibroids (benign growths in the uterus or womb)
4. Contraception
  • Ever used a method to prevent pregnancy
    • Type of method used
    • Primary reason for taking birth control pills
    • When started
    • Duration of use
5. Pregnancy & Obstetric History
  • Ever pregnant
    • Number of pregnancies
    • Age at pregnancies
    • Outcome of pregnancy (e.g., live birth, still birth, miscarriage, etc.)
    • Number of live births
    • Sex of live births
    • Complications of pregnancy — e.g. gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, eclampsia, placenta previa, anemia, depression or anxiety, postpartum depression
    • Breastfeeding
    • Timing of breastfeeding
6. Fertility Treatment
  • Fertility medications used to help get pregnant
    • Type of fertility medication used
7. Menopause Status & Timing
  • Menopausal status
  • Age at menopause
  • Age at final menstrual period
8. Menopause Symptoms
  • Menopause-related symptoms (overall)
  • Vasomotor symptoms
    • Vasomotor symptoms (overall)
    • Hot flashes
    • Night sweats
  • Sleep symptoms
    • Sleep symptoms (overall)
    • Difficulty getting to sleep
    • Difficulty staying asleep
    • Nighttime awakening
  • Somatic symptoms
    • Somatic symptoms (overall)
    • Heart palpitations
    • Skin itching
    • Headaches
    • Bloated stomach
    • Breast tenderness
    • Joint pains
  • Mood symptoms
    • Mood symptoms (overall)
    • Tiredness
    • Irritability
    • Feeling anxious
    • Feeling depressed
    • Mood swings
    • Crying spells
  • Cognitive symptoms
    • Cognitive symptoms (overall)
    • Difficulty concentrating
    • Poor memory
  • Genitourinary symptoms
    • Genitourinary symptoms (overall)
    • Frequent urination
    • Urine leakage
    • Painful urination
    • Bladder infection
    • Stool or gas
    • Dry vagina
    • Vaginal itching
    • Abnormal vaginal discharge
    • Vaginal infection
    • Pain during intercourse
    • Pain inside vagina during intercourse
    • Bleeding after intercourse
  • Sexual/libido symptoms
    • Sexual/libido symptoms (overall)
    • Lack of sexual desire
    • Orgasm difficulty
    • Limited sexual opportunity
  • Symptom detail
    • Severity of symptom
    • Time frame of symptom
9. Perceptions of Menopause
  • Knowledge of menopause (e.g., self-rated knowledge about menopause)
  • Views or perceptions (positive or negative) of menopause
  • Sources of knowledge about menopause
10. Hormone Therapy (HT) Use
  • Hormone therapy use
    • Frequency or duration of use
    • Start / stop age
    • Type
    • Reasons why women start taking hormones (not including birth control pills)
  • Knowledge, views & sources — HT
    • Knowledge of HT (e.g., self-rated knowledge about HT)
    • Views or perceptions (positive or negative) of HT
    • Sources of knowledge about HT
11. Cognitive / Imaging / Biological / Health Record Linked Data
  • Sex hormones
  • Other biomarkers
  • Health records linked
  • Cognitive testing
  • Neuroimaging variables (MRI, fMRI, PET would be most common)
  • Other — any other relevant variables available in a cohort
How to use this

Build one or more conditions below — for example, "N is greater than 1000" or "Hot flashes item: equals Yes". Then choose whether a cohort needs to match all of your conditions (AND) or any of them (OR) using the dropdown. The table updates instantly as you add, edit, or remove conditions, so it's a quick way to answer questions like "which cohorts are publicly available and include a hysterectomy question?" without digging through the raw spreadsheet. Hysterectomy Inference Types is also available as a condition field -- add it to color-code matching rows by type, with a key above the table.
